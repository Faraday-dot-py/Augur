## Results vs predictions

1. **Rho exponent — physics confirmed, estimator biased low.** Exact label and 2nd-order Taylor theory both give slope ~2.0 for all 6 kernels (e.g. analytic 2.05, plw3 2.09-2.10), confirming the theory as predicted. The estimator does not hold to the predicted 1.7-2.1 band for 3 of the 6: analytic 1.57-1.70, yukawa30 1.58-1.65, plw3 1.45-1.48 (all systematically low, worse for steeper laws); inv_distance (1.98-2.03) and plw0.5 (2.02-2.10) pass cleanly; plw1.5 (1.73-1.80) borderline. Confirmed independently by the unbiased read of interp_rho.png: the estimator (red) tracks the exact curve well near rho~1 in every panel, but for analytic/yukawa30/plw1.5/plw3 it sits above (flatter than) the exact curve at small rho — a floor/saturation effect, not present for inv_distance/plw0.5. Net: the estimator learns the correct scaling regime near the training bulk but doesn't extrapolate the curvature down to small rho for steeper-than-1/r force laws. Not what was predicted (expected roughly uniform pass across the first three).

2. **Anisotropy — no collapse, kernel-dependent direction error.** Predicted the estimator would show much less than the true anisotropy ratio (a "collapse toward isotropic"). Instead: analytic/yukawa30/plw3 show the estimator *exaggerating* the elongated/round ratio (e.g. analytic "both" scenario: truth 2.55x, est 3.64x), while inv_distance/plw0.5 get the *sign* wrong (est ratio <1 where truth is >1, i.e. it predicts the round node as stronger). Prediction was wrong on both magnitude and, for half the kernels, direction.

3. **Decoders — confirmed.** Pair-force decoders (pair_p/vec_p/scal_p, direct fit) recover p to ~1e-16 relative error noiseless and <1% at sigma=0.01, for all 5 power laws — exactly as predicted (they are exact fits to noiseless pair forces). The weaker estimator-route decoder (fit p from the estimator's own predicted labels) lands within 10% relative for 4 of 5 power laws (p=1: 5.2%, p=1.5: 5.6%, p=2: 7.9%, p=3: 4.6%; only p=0.5 misses, at 11.8%) — close to the predicted 3-4/5. yukawa30 fit against a plain power-law model is poor (rmse 0.187, expected: model mismatch, not a decoder failure); fit with the matched screened-law (kappa) parameter is accurate.

4. **Symmetries — as predicted, plus a bonus finding.** Exchange and rotation invariance are exactly 0 (built into the features, trivial). Scale covariance breaks by 0.02-0.05 log-units for most kernels at 2x/4x rescale, worse for yukawa30 (0.4-1.9, expected since its screening length sets an absolute scale) — matches the predicted 0.05-0.3 order of magnitude reasonably well. New (job 3025): the whole-state rotation test (0/15/30/45/90 degrees, full 100k-body flyby frame) shows rel_l2 stable at 0.0087-0.0089 for the estimator across all 5 angles (even tighter than the geometric baseline's 0.0171-0.0174), and cost stable at 121-122 vs the baseline's noisier 156-182 — confirms rotation invariance holds end-to-end, not just per-feature.

5. **Granularity map — mostly as predicted, one kernel is the outlier.** Unbiased read: accepted-node-size for the estimator and the geometric baseline track log-density in the same anti-correlated way (fine nodes at the density core, coarse in the sparse envelope) for analytic and inv_distance, closely matching each other in pattern and range. yukawa30 is the outlier: the estimator saturates to coarse acceptance over nearly the whole low-density envelope with a much sharper edge than the geometric rule's smoother falloff — a real, visible learned difference, not present for the other two kernels. Accepted-pairs/particle is a sharp spike at the density core for all three (lower peak count for yukawa30, consistent with its short screening range needing fewer full-resolution pairs). Predicted error maps are diffuse/speckled with no core feature; inv_distance's is flattest/most uniform, yukawa30's is noisiest. No tiling seams or discontinuities in any map.

**New finding not predicted**: cross-kernel substitution in the accept function (using kernel A's estimator on kernel B's dynamics) does not necessarily hurt global force accuracy — in 2 of 6 mismatched pairs it even improved rel_l2 over the matched estimator (e.g. inv_distance dynamics + yukawa30 estimator: rel_l2 0.00095 vs matched 0.00289) — but it inflates compute cost by 100-1000x (23226 or 25120 vs 121-149 matched) and the accepted-node-size ranking becomes anti-correlated with the correct estimator's (spearman -0.26 to -0.32). So a wrong-kernel estimator can still get forces right on average while being far less efficient and picking systematically different (wrong-reasoned) granularity.

**Own-kernel distance generalization (bonus)**: the own-kernel estimator tracks the truth curve within ~0.2 log-units out to 200x the nominal training distance range (tested to dist=510); the same estimator swapped to a mismatched kernel diverges by >1 log-unit at long range. Confirms the estimator is not force-law-invariant, as expected, but also that it isn't merely memorizing the training distance window for its own kernel.

## Bottom line
The estimator captures the qualitative rho^2 scaling, decoder-recoverable structure, and geometric symmetries reasonably well, but is measurably biased at small rho for steep force laws, gets anisotropy direction wrong for about half the tested kernels, and shows one real granularity-map divergence from the geometric rule (yukawa30). None of these are fatal to the "works" claim for adaptive far-field evaluation, but they mean the estimator's local geometric reasoning (size/shape sensitivity) is less reliable than its aggregate accuracy numbers suggest — worth flagging in any interpretability claim in the paper.

## Predictions (written before any real result)
1. Label vs rho = size/dist: exact label and the second-order Taylor theory both scale as rho^2 (smoke check on a fake estimator: truth 2.05, Taylor 2.00). Estimator slope: expected 1.7-2.1 for analytic/inv_distance/yukawa30 on the physical sweep (pass: within 0.3 of 2); less sure for plw0.5 and plw3.
2. Isotropic-node suppression, inv_distance: in the small-A scenario (tiny A, B a ring-pattern disk vs elongated ellipse, same trace) the exact label of the round node should be >10x below the elongated one (Gauss law in 2D, limited by softening eps 0.5 and the ring discretisation); not for p=2. Estimator: I expect it to show much less than 10x (features only see qrr/qtt log ratio, real training pairs are rarely that isotropic, tiny A is out of support). The "both nodes extended" scenario is the in-support one; A's rms spread of the quadratic term does not vanish for round A, so no collapse expected there.
3. Decoders: pair-force and coarse/fine-discrepancy (vector) decoders recover p within 10% for 5/5 power laws without noise (they are exact model fits). Estimator route (fit p to predicted labels): expected 3-4 of 5 within 10%, worse than direct; it adds nothing that pair samples do not already give, since labels are computed from pair forces.
4. Exchange symmetry: exactly 0 (features symmetric). Rotation invariance: exactly 0 by construction (features are projected on the line of centres); pass criterion (<0.1 log-units) met trivially, not a learned property. Scale covariance: not learned, log(dist/EPS) uses a fixed EPS; expect a violation of order 0.05-0.3 log-units at rescale 2-4, removable by feeding dist/eps_kernel.
5. Granularity map: expect accepted node size and pair count to track log density, mostly as the geometric rule does; the estimator-minus-geometric difference is the learned part.
