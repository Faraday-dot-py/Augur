## Q3. Learned corrector designs

### Published analogues (what each actually shows; read = abstract/page fetched this session, search = search summary only)

- Sanchez-Gonzalez et al. 2020 GNS, https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09405 (read abstract): states that the main determinants of long-term performance were mitigating error accumulation by corrupting training data with noise. Abstract only; no details checked.
- Brandstetter et al. 2022 message-passing neural PDE solvers, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03376 (read full text via ar5iv): pushforward trick = unroll 2 steps, backprop only through the last step, with the first step's output used as perturbed input; treats rollout shift as domain adaptation (zero-stability). Reports "far higher survival times"; also that backprop through the first unroll step hurt, and plain Gaussian noise improved stability less than pushforward while reducing accuracy.
- Lippe et al. 2023 PDE-Refiner, https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.05732 (read abstract): attributes long-rollout failure to neglected low-amplitude (often high-frequency) modes; multistep denoising refinement outperforms neural/numerical/hybrid baselines on stability and accuracy. Not N-body; relevant analogy: the SF edge/grid-scale error is a high-frequency component the net under-weights (UNTESTED analogy).
- Um et al. 2020 solver-in-the-loop, https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00016 (read abstract): training with the numerical solver in the loop so the corrector sees the effects of its own earlier corrections; accuracy gains hold over several hundred recurrent steps; beats supervised variants. Closest in spirit to a closed-loop dataset.
- Ross, Gordon, Bagnell 2011 DAgger, https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686 (search): train on states induced by the learner, labelled by the expert; fixes compounding error quadratic in horizon for behaviour cloning.
- Bretherton et al. 2022 (JAMES, doi 10.1029/2021MS002794), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.11820 is the follow-up; the 2022 paper (search): column-wise ML nudging tendencies trained against a 3 km reference correct a 200 km model; 18 h skill gain at 3-7 days, precipitation pattern error -30%; adding wind tendencies helped short range, degraded later. Shows a learned corrector on a frozen coarse model gives modest, biased-based gains and can trade off between variables.
- Greydanus et al. 2019 HNN, https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01563 (read abstract): learns and respects exact conservation in an unsupervised way, demonstrated on the two-body problem and a pendulum; says nothing here about many-body or non-conservative interactions. Hard structure; conflicts with the no-hard-coded-conservation rule.
- Lanzieri, Lanusse, Starck 2022 hybrid physical-neural ODE for PM N-body, https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05509 (read abstract): a Fourier-space network acting on the PM potential adds an effective force compensating PM errors, trained through the ODE solver; power spectrum comparable to PGD, better cross-correlation, robust to resolution/cosmology changes. The closest published analogue to the SF mesh-error corrector (mechanism 7); it is a force-level residual, trained end-to-end through the integrator. Also (search only): Payot et al. 2023 https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.18017 (small-scale PM potential correction generalising to unseen ICs/cosmologies) and COCA https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.02154 (solve in a frame predicted by a CNN so the remaining error is corrected by design).
- Beucler et al. 2021 PRL, https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00912 (search): architecture-level hard constraints enforce conservation to machine precision without degrading performance in their convection emulator, per the abstract; the Physics summary adds that hard constraints make the optimum harder to reach. Hard-constraint option, excluded here.
- Parareal with learned coarse propagators (search only, papers not read): Jin, Lin, Zhou https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.15320; Betcke et al. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09756. They need the fine solver in the loop (assimilation analogue), i.e. not a deployment option for SF.
- ML interatomic potentials (search only, StABlE https://arxiv.org/html/2402.13984v1): neural potentials give unstable simulations that sample unphysical states; stability-aware training on the model's own MD trajectories helps. Same failure class as mechanism 8/9; supports closed-loop training.

I did not find, and did not claim, a published demonstration of a separate learned "energy projector" for N-body surrogates that preserves generalisation.

### Designs

All designs: additive, zero-init output head (matches the existing `kmlp`, `ppmlp`, `gin` pattern), separately logged `a_corr` channel so per-channel ablation stays possible (interpretability), grid-conv based (O(N + G^2), translation-equivariant up to U-Net 2^levels shifts; same tiling caveat as the base).

- L1: residual field head on the existing U-Net with extra diagnostic input channels. Candidate inputs computable from the scatter in O(N): second-moment channel `sum m|v|^2` (local kinetic energy; today only `m, m vx, m vy` are scattered, so a cell with two opposite velocities looks cold: UNTESTED, plausible blind spot behind heating), local density, field divergence. Weak because the net already is a learned residual; adds information, not a new mechanism.
- L2: second small net, frozen base. A small U-Net (width 16, 3 levels) taking the base's inputs plus the base force and diagnostics, outputting additive `a_corr` (every tick) or an additive kick/drift every K ticks. Base weights frozen: attributable, switchable by flag, avoids the fine-tune-drift confound (mechanism 13) and the accuracy trade seen in ft_longpen (+29..76% err when the penalty was trained into the shared weights). Cost: second net pass (contact C already does two passes); tick time rise UNTESTED, expect +30-60%, fails the 5% timing gate like `sub2`.
- L3: learned state projection to an energy shell: needs E target = sim-specific at inference; only legitimate as a soft training loss on `a_corr`. Equivalent to L2 with a penalty; no separate benefit.
- L4: pushforward/noise-trained corrector: train L2 (or the base) with the input state perturbed by the model's own previous-step error (pushforward), or with Gaussian noise of the measured one-step error scale. Noise alone reduced accuracy in MP-PDE; pushforward preferred.
- L5: closed-loop relabelled dataset (DAgger/solver-in-the-loop, chaos-safe). Roll the model for T >> k_end ticks (100-300), snapshot states s_t, then label each by running the EXACT sim for k <= 20 ticks FROM THE MODEL'S OWN STATE (not from the original truth trajectory, which diverges chaotically past the floor). Train the corrector (or base) to match these short truth continuations with BPTT. Short horizon keeps labels inside the chaos floor, states cover the heated/ejected/warm-recurrent-field distribution the model creates (mechanisms 1, 8, 9), and truth is an exact-sim query at training time only (same access as current data generation). Needs the recurrent field and `a_prev` carried in the snapshot (the model's own warm state, never the cold `init_field`).

Risks
- Horizon overfitting is the dominant risk and mechanism 8 is the proof: a head trained to k_end 20 was stable to 20 and exploded at 22-24. A corrector trained on errors up to horizon H will have its own cliff beyond H unless (a) trained on states reached at times >> H (L5), (b) its output is bounded and contractive, or (c) the recurrent state it reads is bounded. Evaluate every candidate to 5x its training horizon.
- Correcting a symptom at the force level (mechanism 7) can lower force error and raise rollout error (prior+pp8: force error 2x better, rollout +73% worse). Always gate on rollout rows.
- Energy-penalty trade-off already measured (ft_longpen).
- A corrector reading velocity can learn sim-specific shortcuts (generalisation); keep the corrector's inputs the same set as the base (no E, no analytic gravity) and test on non-gravity data (the CF/LJ-type families and contact scenes exist).
- Tileability: the corrector is conv-local, but anything with a global reduction (momfix-like, far monopole) breaks strict tiling. Keep corrector output momentum-neutral via the existing momfix applied after it, and evaluate on a domain larger than training (scaling harness rows).

### Evaluating a corrector individually vs the frozen baseline

1. Frozen baseline = Exp E checkpoint, harness `results/baseline_v2_regress.json`; seed 4738 for training, eval 9100/9200/9300/4738, held-out fresh seeds for selection (feedback_select_on_heldout_seeds).
2. One change per run. Controls: (a) same-schedule run with the corrector zero/identity (ft_ctrl analogue) so fine-tune drift is separated; (b) frozen-base corrector isolates (a) automatically.
3. Gate: existing `scatter_regress.py compare` (err@5/10/20 within 15%, dE/dL/dP, bound/virial gaps, no |v|>=c, no non-finite) plus new rows: (i) horizon cliff: max |a| growth per tick from tick k_end to 5 k_end; (ii) cold-vs-warm field invariance; (iii) edge-force probe (square_wall_probe, a(x) at 28-34 vs exact); (iv) contact rows (tunnel, KE ratio, restitution, dE).
4. Judge by channel, not aggregate MSE (feedback_diagnose_rollout_with_channel_breakdown), and against the chaos floors table (WITHIN = at or below the reference-noise floor).
5. After any rollout/video result: fresh hypothesis-free subagent grid review (standing rule).

### Recommendation
- Do not add a separate learned corrector first. The dominant failures are (a) a distribution-shift cliff beyond the training unroll (mechanism 8, 9; and the int_full1/ft_ctrl drift) which a corrector inherits, (b) deterministic geometry artifacts (grid edge, mesh phase) that are fixed by boundary handling or sampling, not by learning, and (c) chaos beyond ~50 ticks that nothing can fix.
- Order: (1) oracle-reset diagnostic D1-D3 (cheap, decides everything else); (2) edge guard band + re-probe far@0.05 (traditional, no retrain, prerequisite for tiling); (3) closed-loop relabelled fine-tune with same-schedule control; (4) only if D2 shows state/energy error drives later position error, a frozen-base residual corrector trained with the same loop.
- All of these keep sim-specific energy/L formulas out of the model; the oracle experiments use them as diagnostics only.
